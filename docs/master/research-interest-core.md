# Core Research-Interest Note

## Working title

**Reliable Computational Systems for Heterogeneous Biological Evidence**

## Research identity

My research interest is in building reliable computational methods and systems that turn complex biological measurements into reproducible and useful scientific evidence.

My background began in Mechatronics Engineering, where I developed a systems view of sensing, measurement, control and software. I then worked with physiological and neural signals in wearable-neurotechnology R&D, gained exposure to medical imaging, and later moved into genomics R&D and open-source scientific software for biological-data analysis.

These experiences span different modalities, but the recurring engineering problem has been consistent:

```text
measurement / biological evidence
        ->
representation and quality control
        ->
modelling / inference
        ->
software / computational system
        ->
validation
        ->
scientific or clinical use
```

I now want research training that makes this common layer more rigorous through statistical inference, modelling, optimization, algorithms, scientific computing and substantial thesis work.

## Research principles

### 1. Measurement and data generation matter

Biological data are not abstract matrices.

Signals, images and sequencing data inherit limitations from:

- sensing / acquisition;
- assay design;
- sampling;
- preprocessing;
- measurement noise;
- technical artifacts.

I am especially interested in methods that model these constraints rather than hiding them behind an opaque prediction pipeline.

### 2. Robust inference matters more than benchmark accuracy

Real biological data are:

- heterogeneous;
- incomplete;
- small or expensive to label;
- affected by domain shift;
- biologically and technically confounded.

I want to study methods that explicitly address uncertainty, missing evidence and generalization.

### 3. Software is part of scientific methodology

Scientific software should preserve evidence, expose assumptions, support reproducibility and make validation auditable.

My open-source DNA work has strengthened my interest in the boundary between biological claims and the computational systems that produce them.

## Adaptable research kernels

The following are **related methodological directions**, not four fixed thesis proposals.

### Kernel A - Robust multimodal biological inference

**Question**

How can computational models integrate heterogeneous biological modalities while remaining reliable when modalities are incomplete, misaligned or collected under different workflows?

Example modalities:

- pathology / medical images;
- genomics / omics;
- clinical variables.

Methods to deepen:

- multimodal representation learning;
- uncertainty;
- missing-modality learning;
- domain generalization;
- clinically/scientifically aligned validation.

Best current fit:

- Hao Chen / HKUST;
- Yueming Jin / NUS.

### Kernel B - Assay-aware biological data systems

**Question**

How can computational representations and reproducible workflows incorporate the structure and technical uncertainty of biological assays rather than treating sequencing outputs as context-free data?

Example settings:

- single-cell sequencing;
- longitudinal molecular measurements;
- multi-omics.

Methods to deepen:

- statistical modelling;
- batch / technical-effect modelling;
- data representation;
- reproducible scientific computing;
- experimental-computational integration.

Best current fit:

- Cheow Lih Feng / NUS;
- Jiguang Wang / HKUST.

### Kernel C - Physics-informed computational inference

**Question**

How can acquisition physics and data-driven models be combined to recover reliable information from limited or shifted biomedical measurements?

Example settings:

- MRI / CT;
- computational imaging;
- inverse imaging problems.

Methods to deepen:

- inverse problems;
- optimization;
- signal processing;
- physics-informed learning;
- uncertainty / domain shift.

Best current fit:

- Bihan Wen / NTU.

### Kernel D - Measurement-aware physiological inference

**Question**

How can multimodal physiological measurements be fused into robust state estimates under real-world sensor noise, missing channels and physiological variability?

Example signals:

- ECG;
- PPG;
- bioimpedance;
- wearable physiological streams.

Methods to deepen:

- sensor fusion;
- physiological modelling;
- system identification;
- uncertainty;
- longitudinal validation.

Best current fit:

- Daniel Franklin / Toronto.

## What I am not optimizing for

I am not seeking a Master's merely to add another biological domain or another AI credential.

I am also not trying to become:

- a pure molecular biologist;
- a generic software engineer;
- a modality-specific AI practitioner.

The long-term goal is to become a **Research Engineer for biological systems** who can move among different forms of biological evidence while bringing a rigorous engineering, computational and validation mindset.

## Desired Master's outcome

The Master's should make the following transition possible:

```text
Mechatronics + signals + imaging + genomics + scientific software
                         ->
rigorous modelling / statistics / algorithms / scientific computing
                         ->
Research Engineer for Biological Systems
```

A successful thesis would therefore make a substantive methodological contribution while remaining grounded in a real biological or healthcare research problem.
