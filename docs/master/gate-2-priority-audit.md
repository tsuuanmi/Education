# Gate 2 Priority Audit - 2027 Application Portfolio

This document is the detailed decision layer on top of `gate-2-funding.md`.

It combines:

- Gate-1 formal eligibility;
- funding structure and residual cost;
- how automatic/structural vs competitive the funding is;
- research/career fit with the Research Engineer goal;
- application friction and test burden;
- deadline urgency for the 2027 intake.

It deliberately does **not** assign fake admission probabilities. The ratings below are relative application priorities.

## Applicant-specific lens

The strongest evidence in the current profile is not just "BME interest":

- Mechatronics engineering foundation;
- control, electronics, sensing, measurement and programming;
- A+ EEG thesis;
- wearable-neurotechnology R&D;
- genomics R&D and public research outputs;
- scientific-software engineering;
- research award and scholarships.

That makes methods-first research programmes in ECE/EE/sensing/control especially valuable: they preserve neurotechnology fit while keeping genomics and broader scientific-engineering optionality.

## Why Hong Kong moves to the front

Hong Kong research programmes are unusually attractive because the studentship is structurally tied to full-time research postgraduate study rather than being a completely separate external-scholarship lottery.

### CUHK BME MPhil

2027/28 BME tuition is HK$49,500/year. CUHK's university-level Postgraduate Studentship is available to qualified full-time research postgraduate students; the 2026/27 annual amount is HK$229,200 and results are normally announced with admission.

The BME programme itself requests exactly the evidence this profile can supply well: CV, research outputs, research-interest statement, awards/certificates and class ranking.

Research fit is direct:

- wearable sensors and mobile health;
- BCI/EEG;
- biomedical instrumentation;
- medical robotics;
- biosensors.

**Decision: P0.** The 1 Dec 2026 main round should be treated as a real execution deadline, not a research deadline.

Sources:
- https://www.gs.cuhk.edu.hk/programmes/engineering/mphil-phd-biomedical-engineering
- https://www.gs.cuhk.edu.hk/admissions/scholarships-fees/studentships
- https://www.bme.cuhk.edu.hk/new/bdb.php

### HKU ECE MPhil

HKU's 2027/28 main research-postgraduate round closes 1 Dec 2026. Full-time MPhil/PhD students with a first degree equivalent to Second Class Honours Division I or above are normally considered for PGS; the base rate is HK$19,135/month from Sep 2026, subject to funding and annual renewal.

2027/28 composition fee is HK$49,500/year, so a PGS award leaves a meaningful gross amount for living after tuition.

The main uncertainty is not domain fit; it is whether the UET Distinction / GPA 3.47/4 is treated as the required honours equivalent.

**Decision: P0, but explicitly document degree-class equivalence.**

Sources:
- https://gradsch.hku.hk/prospective_students/application/application_period
- https://gradsch.hku.hk/prospective_students/fees_scholarships_and_financial_support/postgraduate_scholarships
- https://gradsch.hku.hk/prospective_students/admitted_students

### HKUST ECE MPhil

HKUST ECE is arguably the cleanest methods-first research fit in the current universe.

Official research areas combine:

- neuroengineering;
- biosignal processing;
- medical electronics;
- bioinformatics/computational biology;
- detection and estimation;
- networked sensing and control;
- medical/healthcare control applications.

The 2027/28 tuition fee is HK$49,500/year. HKUST's 2027/28 prospectus states PGS of HK$229,620/year for up to two years for a full-time MPhil. Non-local ECE Fall 2027 deadline is 1 Jun 2027 and admission is rolling.

**Decision: P0. Apply well before the formal deadline because rolling admission makes June a misleading planning date.**

Sources:
- https://ece.hkust.edu.hk/research/areas
- https://prog-crs.hkust.edu.hk/pgprog/2027-28/mphil-phd-ece
- https://ebookshelf.hkust.edu.hk/flippingbook/G23842_PG_Prospectus/16-17/

## Singapore: best package, but funding is a competitive layer

### NUS ECE / BME MEng Research

For August 2027 research programmes, international applications close **15 Jan 2027**. Applicants must propose a thesis advisor and research area.

The NUS Research Scholarship for an international Master's (Research) student currently provides:

- full tuition waiver;
- S$2,900/month;
- typical duration two years;
- no specific service bond;
- Graduate Assistantship duties.

NUS's own graduate-school estimate puts a typical monthly cost around S$1,200-S$2,100 on campus, so the stipend is financially viable if the scholarship is awarded.

The important distinction is that scholarship applicants are expected to meet at least Second Class Upper-equivalent academic quality. The current GPA/classification should therefore be presented together with the A+ thesis, research outputs, awards and R&D evidence rather than as GPA alone.

Between the two routes, **ECE is slightly preferred** because it preserves a wider methods identity while still supporting medical instrumentation, neuroengineering, wearable healthcare and signal processing. BME is an excellent second application if there is a clearly distinct supervisor/project.

**Decision: NUS ECE P0; NUS BME P1.**

Sources:

- https://nusgs.nus.edu.sg/scholarships/nus-research-scholarship
- https://nusgs.nus.edu.sg/cost-of-living/
- https://cde.nus.edu.sg/ece/research/research-areas/ices/
- https://cde.nus.edu.sg/bme/graduate/admission-requirements/

### NTU EEE MEng Research

NTU EEE is another unusually strong profile match. Current EEE research strengths include:

- biomedical signal/image processing;
- bioelectronics and biomedical devices;
- neurotechnology;
- control theory;
- machine learning/optimization;
- advanced sensing;
- multimodal sensor fusion;
- smart sensors and wearable devices.

The NTU Research Scholarship for an international research Master's currently covers annual tuition and S$2,900/month for up to two years, with Graduate Assistantship duties and no stated service bond.

The risk is the overseas-degree threshold: NTU research schools use Upper Second / Honours Distinction equivalence, and some schools strongly encourage or require GRE for overseas applicants. A strong GRE would therefore have unusually high marginal value for this application even if the EEE page does not present it as a universal hard gate.

**Decision: P0. Build the application around a concrete EEE PI and a methods-first research proposal, not a generic BME statement.**

Sources:

## Korea: strongest cost-to-funding economics

### KAIST Bio & Brain

KAIST is one of the strongest overall bets.

KAIST states that its international graduate scholarship covers full tuition, health insurance and a stipend, and that about 94% of international students benefit. Master's applicants are automatically considered for KGPS when they choose KAIST Scholarship; KGPS gives full tuition + KRW 1,000,000/month for four semesters.

KAIST's official cost estimate for a single graduate student is about KRW 5.35m per six months excluding tuition; this makes a KRW 1m/month stipend broadly viable on campus. Bio & Brain also has unusually direct lab fit: current work includes brain-machine interfaces, EEG/ECG wearable systems, neural recording/stimulation and real-time signal processing.

Fall 2027 dates are still TBA, but KAIST says the normal Fall Early cycle is early-to-late December.

**Decision: P0. Begin lab mapping now; do not wait for the Fall 2027 announcement.**

Sources:
- https://admission.kaist.ac.kr/intl-graduate/FinancialSupport/Scholarship/KAISTScholarship/
- https://admission.kaist.ac.kr/intl-graduate/Admission/YearlyTimelines
- https://beee.kaist.ac.kr/
- https://admission.kaist.ac.kr/intl-graduate/FinancialSupport/CostofAttendance

### POSTECH EE / I-Bio

POSTECH's 2026 regular Master's TA/RA package is KRW 1.85m/month total: KRW 884k tuition component + KRW 966k living allowance. The university explicitly notes that actual amounts vary by lab and advisors may add funding.

This package is credible against POSTECH's own living-cost estimate of about US$715/month, with graduate dormitory rooms from roughly KRW 149k-267k/month.

For this profile, **EE should rank above I-Bio** unless a specific I-Bio PI is an exceptional fit. EE keeps control, circuits, sensing and signal methods central; I-Bio may pull the narrative toward bioscience.

The latest international guide found covers 2026 admissions and Spring 2027; the Fall 2027 guide is not yet published.

**Decision: EE P1-high; I-Bio P1-secondary.**

Sources:
- https://adm-g.postech.ac.kr/ENG/graduate-info/support-policy/
- https://postech.ac.kr/eng/student-life/dormitory.do
- https://international.postech.ac.kr/

## Erasmus Mundus: excellent economics, low robustness

An EMJM scholarship is financially superb: participation costs are covered and the EU unit scholarship is EUR 1,400/month for up to 24 months.

But these should be treated as **high-upside bets, not financial safeties**.

EMJM Imaging is a useful calibration point: its 2026 first round received 1,108 applications, while the programme states it offers around 15 scholarships per year. The raw numbers show how selective the funding layer is even before considering eligibility and ranking details.

### EMJM Imaging

Strong methods fit in imaging/signal processing/ML. The 2027 application dates are not yet posted; the 2026 scholarship round ran 17 Nov-9 Jan.

**Decision: P1. Apply, but do not count it as expected funding.**

Sources:
- https://www.emjm-imaging.eu/application-and-admission/
- https://www.emjm-imaging.eu/costs-and-scholarship/

### MAIA

The 2026-28 edition was not offered, but the consortium has now secured renewed Erasmus Mundus funding for the 2027-29 edition. The official site says the new cohort is expected Sep 2027 and tentatively places the scholarship application window from Nov 2026 to Feb 2027.

**Decision: P1. Strong biomedical-AI hedge; wait for the actual call before modelling exact funding.**

Source:
- https://maiamaster.udg.edu/admission/

### IPCVAI

The 2027-29 call is explicitly announced for 1 Nov 2026-2 Feb 2027. The programme states a cohort of 25 and more than 1,600 applications in its current admission description.

Excellent methods training, but less directly tied to neuro/physiological sensing than the research-degree frontier.

**Decision: P1-low. Worth applying if the reusable EMJM package makes marginal effort small.**

Source:
- https://ipcv.eu/

### EMMaH

The curriculum fit is unusually good for research-to-product biomedical engineering, but the currently published admission page is still about the 2026-28 cohort and its self-funded second phase. Do not yet assume a 2027 scholarship call/package.

**Decision: monitor; keep high priority if the 2027 scholarship call is confirmed.**

Source:
- https://www.master-emmah.eu/application-and-admission/admission-and-selection

## TU Delft SNS: scholarship-only logic

The Microelectronics department states that some scholarships cover full MSc tuition and contribute to living expenses for two years, and that the complete MSc application should be uploaded before **1 Dec** for scholarship consideration.

This is highly attractive for the Signals, Networking and Sensing track, but the asymmetry is important:

- with scholarship: excellent fit + viable finance;
- without scholarship: high non-EU cost and no reason to prefer it over funded Asian research routes.

**Decision: P1 as a scholarship-conditioned application. Do not plan to self-fund.**

Source:
- https://microelectronics.tudelft.nl/Education/detail.php?ti=12

## SNU: good university, weaker funding certainty

For Spring 2027, GSFS offered full tuition for up to four semesters plus at least KRW 500,000/month for Master's students for at least one year, with continuation depending on the college/school. Only 22 students were selected university-wide for that Spring 2027 call, and the deadline was already 9 Jul 2026.

This makes SNU financially less robust than KAIST/POSTECH for the current plan. It remains worth targeting for Fall 2027 if Bioengineering lab fit is exceptional, but should not displace P0 applications.

**Decision: P1, Fall 2027.**

Sources:
- https://oga.snu.ac.kr/scholarship_notice-board/?mod=document&uid=574
- https://en-cdn.snu.ac.kr/admission/graduate/scholarships/before_application

## University of Tokyo G30: excellent but test-heavy

The School of Engineering currently lists the International Bioengineering Course under G30 for October 2027. Bioengineering guidance requires TOEFL/IELTS and GRE General, and preliminary selection uses GPA, study plan, language score, GRE and recommendations.

MEXT-Bio priority placement exists, but nomination should be treated as competitive rather than assumed.

Because both GRE and English scores are hard inputs, this route has a higher preparation burden than Hong Kong/Korea.

**Decision: P1 only if the testing timeline is feasible.**

Sources:
- https://www.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/en/study-at-utokyo/soe/international-programs
- https://bioeng.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/admission/?lang=en

## Current conclusion

The programme universe should now be mentally compressed to three layers:

```text
Primary research frontier
  HKUST ECE
  CUHK BME
  HKU ECE
  NUS ECE/BME
  NTU EEE
  KAIST BBE
  POSTECH EE

Scholarship upside
  TU Delft SNS
  EMJM Imaging / MAIA / EMMaH / IPCVAI
  UTokyo G30 + MEXT
  SNU Bioengineering

Hedges
  Taiwan research
  Italy/Germany low-cost
```

The key strategic change is that **funding robustness now matters more than nominal scholarship size**. A programme with a structurally funded research studentship is usually a better financial target than a high-cost MSc with a spectacular but very low-probability merit scholarship.
