# Geography / Environment Preference Policy

## Purpose

Academic fit is not sufficient for a Master's decision.

A Master's is a 1-2 year lived research environment. Country, institutional culture, language, day-to-day life, and the applicant's own willingness to live and work there materially affect expected value.

Therefore **personal/environment fit is a separate decision dimension** and must not be overwritten by academic ranking or funding.

## Current applicant preference

As of 2026-10-04:

> The applicant does not currently feel that Korea or Japan are personally suitable environments for Master's study.

This is treated as a real strategic preference, not as noise to be optimized away.

## Decision rule

Korea and Japan remain in the canonical opportunity universe for provenance and optionality.

However, they are **not part of the default application core** unless one of the following happens:

1. the applicant's preference changes;
2. a uniquely exceptional project / PI creates enough upside to justify reconsideration;
3. a specific short-term opportunity has unusually low application cost and the applicant explicitly wants to keep it alive.

Do not spend scarce application bandwidth, testing effort, recommendation capital, or PI-outreach effort on Korea/Japan by default.

## Separation of scores

Do not downgrade the academic facts.

For example:

- UNIST BME can remain academically Very-high;
- KAIST Bio & Brain can remain academically P0-quality;
- NAIST Computational Biology can remain a strong methods fit.

But portfolio priority must include an independent environment-fit dimension.

Conceptually:

```text
Academic fit        != Personal/environment fit
Funding fit         != Personal/environment fit
Prestige            != Personal/environment fit

Final application priority
  = academic/research value
  x financial feasibility
  x execution feasibility
  x personal/environment willingness
```

A route with near-zero willingness should not remain core merely because the first three terms are strong.
