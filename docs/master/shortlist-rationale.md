# Shortlist Rationale - Why Other Programmes Are Not Core

This document explains why programmes outside the current seven-programme core portfolio were deprioritised.

The key distinction is:

- **Dropped from the universe**: formal eligibility or cycle structure is bad enough that application effort is not justified.
- **Removed from the core shortlist**: still a valid option, but dominated by another programme on funding robustness, research fit, application friction, or timing.
- **Hedge / upside bet**: intentionally retained, but not allowed to displace the core research applications.

## Decision rule

A programme is not core when another option offers an equal or better combination of:

1. formal eligibility;
2. research/lab fit;
3. funding robustness;
4. residual cost;
5. application friction;
6. timing;
7. option value for the Research Engineer trajectory.

This is not a prestige ranking.

## 1. Strong programmes dominated financially

### Aalto Health Technology

Why not core:
- strong Mechatronics/sensing fit;
- Aalto Excellence is a tuition-waiver scholarship, not a living stipend;
- living therefore remains a hard self-funding exposure;
- research-degree routes in Hong Kong/Korea/Singapore offer comparable or better technical fit with stronger living support.

Decision: **keep only as scholarship/finance-dependent backup**.

### KTH Systems, Control and Robotics

Why not core:
- excellent methods fit;
- KTH Scholarship covers tuition but not living;
- scholarship selection itself is highly competitive;
- therefore a funded HKUST/NTU/NUS/KAIST route dominates it financially while preserving the same control/estimation identity.

Decision: **excellent academic backup, weak core financial choice**.

### Oulu / Tampere

Why not core:
- academic fit is clean;
- 2027 Finnish funding is structurally partial;
- living costs are not covered by tuition scholarships;
- Tampere states only a very small number of tuition scholarships are awarded university-wide.

Decision: **do not spend early application bandwidth unless funding improves**.

### TUM Biomedical Engineering and Medical Physics

Why not core:
- research quality is high;
- programme is more physics/imaging-heavy than the current neural-sensing/control trajectory;
- current third-country tuition is EUR 6,000/semester;
- application includes a dedicated aptitude examination.

Decision: **dominated on cost + friction + fit by core research routes**.

### FAU Medical Robotics

Why not core:
- excellent robotics/engineering fit;
- EUR 4,000/semester tuition from summer 2027 weakens the financial case;
- funding is not as structural as Asian research degrees.

Decision: **backup only if a specific scholarship or lab changes the economics**.

## 2. Financially sensible, but weaker than the research frontier

### Luebeck / Freiburg / Heidelberg

Why not core:
- financially resilient because tuition is low or absent;
- good academic fit;
- but primarily coursework Master's routes without a built-in living stipend;
- lab/supervisor alignment is less central to admission than in MPhil/MEng/MS research programmes.

Decision: **important safety layer, not primary research frontier**.

### Politecnico di Milano / Bologna / Genoa

Why not core:
- Italy has unusually useful merit and need-based mechanisms;
- Polimi can offer full tuition waiver + allowance; Bologna ER.GO can materially reduce net cost; IYT creates another route;
- however funding is still award/means-test dependent rather than structurally attached to research admission;
- programmes remain mainly taught Master's rather than funded PI-driven research degrees.

Decision: **keep one Italy hedge, not all three in the core**.

### BME Paris

Why not core:
- good interdisciplinary neuro/BME fit;
- Eiffel can be strong;
- but Eiffel requires institution nomination and is not automatic;
- programme is a taught Master's route, not a structurally funded research degree.

Decision: **strong scholarship hedge**.

## 3. Good research/funding but redundant with a better sibling route

### NUS BME MEng Research

Why not core-seven:
- objectively strong;
- full NUS Research Scholarship route exists;
- but NUS ECE preserves a broader methods identity while still supporting neuroengineering, sensing and biomedical instrumentation;
- applying to BME makes sense only if there is a distinct PI/project.

Decision: **P1 duplicate, not rejected**.

### HKUST Bioengineering MPhil

Why not core-seven:
- same strong PGS economics;
- good BME research;
- HKUST ECE currently has denser fit across BMI, biosignal processing, estimation/control and neuroengineering.

Decision: **apply only for a clearly different PI**.

### NTU CCEB MEng Research

Why not core-seven:
- same excellent NTU Research Scholarship structure;
- current biomedical scope is relatively more biotechnology/tissue-engineering heavy;
- NTU EEE maps more directly to wearable/neural sensing, signals and systems.

Decision: **funded alternative, lower fit**.

### POSTECH Interdisciplinary Bioscience and Bioengineering

Why not core-seven:
- same attractive TA/RA ecosystem;
- but POSTECH EE now has a very strong direct PI match in Sungmin Park;
- EE keeps control/electronics/sensing central while still reaching neuro/medical devices.

Decision: **secondary only if a standout I-Bio PI appears**.

## 4. Strong but funding less predictable

### SNU Bioengineering

Why not core:
- research fit is strong;
- GSFS/GKS/SNU Global can fund study;
- package duration and availability are less predictable than KAIST scholarship or POSTECH TA/RA;
- some scholarship calls select only a small number university-wide.

Decision: **P1, worth applying for Fall 2027, but not a funding safety**.

### University of Tokyo G30 Bioengineering

Why not core:
- strong research reputation and good BME fit;
- GRE General plus TOEFL/IELTS are required;
- MEXT university/priority-placement funding is nomination-based and competitive;
- substantially higher test/application friction than Hong Kong/Korea.

Decision: **P1 only if GRE + English testing plan is already justified by other applications**.

### Tohoku Biomedical Engineering

Why not core:
- academically relevant;
- prospective-advisor permission and entrance examination create additional procedural gates;
- funding route is less clear/structural than KAIST/POSTECH/HK research options.

Decision: **keep only after supervisor/funding confirmation**.

## 5. Taiwan: excellent hedge, but not yet the primary frontier

Programmes:
- NTU Smart Medicine and Health Informatics;
- NTU Biomedical Electronics and Bioinformatics;
- NTU Biomedical Engineering;
- NTHU Biomedical Engineering;
- NTHU BMES;
- NYCU Intelligent Bioelectrical Engineering.

Why not core:
- eligibility and thematic fit are generally good;
- Taiwan Scholarship is attractive but includes a tuition cap rather than universal full tuition;
- university scholarships are often annual/renewable and can be budget or advisor dependent;
- package predictability is therefore lower than HK PGS / KAIST / POSTECH;
- current PI-level audit has not yet shown the same density of exceptional direct matches as HKUST/KAIST/NTU Singapore.

Decision: **keep one or two Taiwan applications as serious financial/research hedges**.

## 6. Erasmus Mundus: upside bets, not safeties

Programmes:
- EMJM Imaging;
- MAIA;
- EMMaH;
- IPCVAI;
- EMMBIOME;
- MICROSCOM.

Why not core:
- scholarship economics are excellent;
- but scholarship selection is a separate highly competitive layer;
- these are joint taught Master's programmes rather than PI-driven research degrees;
- therefore their expected funding robustness is lower even when the nominal package is larger.

Decision:
- **apply selectively using a reusable package**;
- never count an EMJM scholarship as expected funding until awarded.

## 7. TU Delft: excellent only under scholarship-conditioned logic

### EE - Signals, Networking and Sensing

Why not core:
- excellent methods fit;
- department scholarships can be very strong;
- without one, non-EU self-funding is expensive;
- therefore the programme is dominated by funded research routes unless scholarship is won.

Decision: **scholarship-only P1 application**.

### BME - Medical Devices

Why below SNS:
- good device/sensing fit;
- SNS better preserves the methods-first identity and optionality;
- same scholarship dependency.

Decision: **secondary Delft choice**.

## 8. High-prestige moonshots removed by formal-risk logic

### ETH Zurich Biomedical Engineering

Why not core:
- exceptional programme and ESOP upside;
- non-EU/EFTA applicants require GRE with high expected scores;
- Mechatronics is not as clean a listed qualifying discipline as EE/ME;
- application effort is high and eligibility/selectivity uncertainty remains.

Decision: **moonshot only if GRE preparation is already happening**.

### EPFL Life Sciences Engineering

Why not core:
- intellectual fit is excellent;
- engineering side matches well;
- EPFL explicitly requires cell/molecular-biology foundations;
- professional genomics experience cannot safely be assumed to replace formal coursework.

Decision: **formal-risk dominated**.

## 9. Hard-credit programmes

These are not core because formal prerequisite risk appears before fit or funding.

### Chalmers BME
Hard requirement: >=6 credits Signals & Systems / Signal Processing including linear systems and transforms.

### KTH Medical Engineering
Hard requirement: >=15 ECTS Physics.

### DTU BME
International applicants need 65 ECTS distributed across explicit baskets including chemistry, human physiology/anatomy/cell biology, and 15 ECTS BME.

### RWTH BME
Formal life-science/chemistry content appears mismatched.

### RWTH EEITCE - Biomedical Systems Engineering
Hard theoretical-EE and systems-theory credit baskets remain uncertain.

### Bergamo Medical Engineering
Requires formal Bioengineering + Medical Disciplines credits.

### KIT BME
Admission ranking rewards prior BME content heavily, making the current transcript structurally weaker.

### Europhotonics
Physics-credit requirement remains unresolved.

Decision: **resolve only if a transcript/syllabus audit can prove the basket; otherwise drop**.

## 10. Preparatory-program risk

Programmes:
- Twente BME/PSS;
- TU Eindhoven BME;
- KU Leuven BME;
- Ghent BME;
- VUB BME;
- ULB BME;
- Groningen BME.

Why not core:
- Mechatronics may be relevant intellectually;
- but direct-entry equivalence is uncertain and a preparatory/pre-Master route may be imposed;
- that creates extra time, cost, and in some cases scholarship ineligibility;
- this is a poor trade when cleaner engineering-first programmes exist.

Decision: **do not pay application-effort tax unless a pre-assessment confirms direct entry**.

## 11. Domain/language mismatch

### Freiburg Neuroscience
Strong EEG/neurotechnology content, but narrower neuroscience identity than the desired degree-title-agnostic Research Engineer path.

### Padua Bioengineering
Language structure is not cleanly all-English.

### Paris-Saclay E3A -> ATSI
Excellent control/signal fit, but M1 language path creates a practical French-language constraint.

### Neurasmus / IMIM
Excellent neuroscience/medicine brands, but substantially more biology-heavy than the current formal background.

Decision: **fit penalty or execution penalty, not programme-quality judgment**.

## 12. Australia - now a serious 2028 branch

Australia was previously treated mainly as a biomedical-engineering fallback. That is too narrow after the research-identity correction.

Australia Awards is unusually important because it can cover full tuition, living support, travel, OSHC and research-related support for a Master's of up to two years. The 2027 round is closed; 2028 terms are not yet published and must be refreshed when the next call opens.

Current high-value Australia routes now include two distinct subtracks:

**Systems / biomedical engineering**
- UNSW MPhil Engineering - Biomedical Engineering;
- Monash Master of Engineering Science (Research);
- UNSW Master of Engineering Science - Biomedical Engineering.

**Computational genetics / genomics**
- UQ Master of Bioinformatics (Research Extensive);
- Monash Master of Bioinformatics - Research Stream;
- UNSW MPhil BME with Digital Medicine & Digital Biology / computational genomics direction.

The Australia Awards two-year return-to-Vietnam rule remains a real career constraint. It can nevertheless become strategically coherent if the post-study plan is framed around building computational genomics / biomedical-data infrastructure, open scientific software and translational R&D in Vietnam.

Decision: **build a separate 2028 Australia portfolio rather than treating Australia as a backup country.**

## 13. Not actionable / data hygiene

### Grenoble SIGMA
Excellent methodological fit but no normal 2027 entry path.

### Osaka generic English Master's row
Not a concrete division/lab/programme, so not scoreable.

### Superseded rows
Old generic NTU Singapore / NTU Taiwan / NYCU records were replaced by exact programme rows.

Decision: **exclude mechanically, not strategically**.

## What "core seven" really means

The current seven are not necessarily the seven globally best universities.

They are the seven that currently sit closest to the applicant-specific Pareto frontier:

```text
strong formal eligibility
+ multiple plausible PIs
+ research-degree structure
+ strong funding
+ low residual cost
+ direct continuity from Mechatronics -> quantitative biological evidence -> Research Engineer
```

The current neuro/sensing core remains useful, but it is **not yet the final cross-domain core** because PI-level auditing has so far been much deeper for neurotechnology than for computational genetics/genomics.

The next step is to build a parallel computational-genomics supervisor/programme frontier and then recombine both branches.
