# Tier-0 PI Deep Audit - UNIST and NAIST

## Purpose

This is the first deep pass after the broad PI frontier.

The goal is to move from:

> "This PI seems thematically relevant"

to:

> "There is a credible applicant-specific research pitch, the lab is visibly active, and the methods would deepen the target Research Engineer identity."

## UNIST

### 1. Min Hyuk Lim - highest-priority UNIST contact

Current UNIST materials describe the Intelligence and Control-based BioMedicine lab as "code-to-clinic" research using data-driven and knowledge-based models.

The methodological stack is unusually close to the applicant's desired Master's depth:

- statistics;
- control;
- optimization;
- machine learning / AI;
- time-series and medical data;
- prediction and inference;
- decision-making and implementation.

This is stronger than merely saying the lab works on "digital healthcare."

#### Credible research pitch

A plausible pitch is:

> **Uncertainty-aware and control-informed modelling for noisy biomedical time-series, with reusable QC / representation / validation pipelines for clinical decision support.**

Why this pitch is credible:

- Earable provides real physiological-signal experience;
- Mechatronics/control gives a systems foundation;
- GeneStory/DNA demonstrates data-pipeline and reproducibility maturity;
- the proposal is methodological rather than locked to sleep/EEG.

The proposal should avoid sounding like "I want to continue my old wearable project."

The stronger framing is:

> I have repeatedly worked with noisy biological evidence in different modalities and want to develop principled methods for inference and decision-making under measurement uncertainty.

**Decision: contact first.**

### 2. Kyemyung Park - strongest cross-modality modelling route

The Systems ImmunoDynamics lab builds predictive dynamical models of the immune system.

Current work emphasizes:

- multiscale biological modelling;
- multi-omics;
- clinical data;
- images;
- ML;
- Bayesian statistics;
- mathematical modelling.

A 2025 paper/perspective specifically discusses multi-physiology modelling and precision immunotherapy.

#### Credible research pitch

> **Reproducible multiscale inference across heterogeneous patient evidence, integrating omics / clinical / imaging information with explicit uncertainty and validation.**

The applicant should not pretend to be an immunologist.

The value proposition is instead:

- systems thinking;
- computational modelling;
- multi-modal data integration;
- scientific software;
- biological-domain learning.

This is exactly the type of lab where missing biology can become productive learning rather than a reason to reject the prior profile.

**Decision: contact second.**

### 3. Semin Lee - strongest genomics continuity

The Computational Biology Lab is clearly active: its public member roster was updated in September 2026 and includes research professors, postdocs, MS/PhD students and undergraduates.

Research focuses on:

- cancer genomics;
- multi-omics;
- germline/somatic alterations;
- somatic mosaicism;
- heterogeneity/evolution;
- single-cell genomics.

#### Credible research pitch

> **Robust and reproducible analysis of low-frequency / heterogeneous genomic variation across samples, with explicit evidence modelling, QC and software pipelines.**

This can naturally leverage:

- GeneStory genomic R&D;
- the DNA open-source scientific-software platform;
- experience with variant calling / nomenclature / evidence representation;
- large Vietnamese mtDNA and sequencing-data experience where relevant.

The risk is not fit; the risk is becoming genomics-only.

The outreach should therefore emphasize **methods and scientific software**, not simply "I already work in genomics."

**Decision: contact third.**

## NAIST

### Important correction: Koichi Fujiwara is scholarship-actionable

The 2027 MEXT IPGP call is for the **Division of Information Science**, not only laboratories explicitly listed on the older Computational Biology programme page.

The current guideline says applicants should contact the Division of Information Science laboratory they wish to enter.

Biomedical Data Science is a current Division of Information Science laboratory.

Therefore Koichi Fujiwara is a legitimate PI to approach for the 2027 IPGP route, subject to the professor agreeing to supervise and write the Letter of Acceptance.

The scholarship has only **4 Master's slots**, and the application period is **14 October-13 November 2026**.

Only one faculty member can be asked for the Letter of Acceptance at a time.

### 1. Koichi Fujiwara - highest-priority NAIST contact

This is one of the strongest applicant-specific matches found in the entire search.

The lab's philosophy explicitly addresses **small biomedical data**, arguing that real clinical data often require:

- cleaning;
- format alignment;
- expert knowledge;
- physiology / physics priors;
- careful dataset construction;
- medical-device / diagnostic-algorithm development.

Current domains include epilepsy, sleep disorder, stroke and other clinical signals.

Fujiwara's current profile lists:

- mathematical modelling;
- ML;
- biosignal processing;
- medical AI;
- neuroscience;
- process systems engineering.

#### Credible research pitch

> **Reliable small-data physiological inference for wearable and clinical signals using expert/physiology priors, uncertainty-aware ML, rigorous QC and reproducible benchmarking.**

This is unusually credible because the applicant already has:

- wearable physiological sensing;
- raw-signal processing;
- engineering/system modelling;
- software pipeline experience.

The proposal should explicitly say that the goal is **not another sleep classifier**.

The deeper research question is:

> how to make biomedical inference reliable when data are noisy, limited, heterogeneous and expensive to label.

That is portable far beyond Earable.

**Decision: first NAIST contact.**

### 2. Yoshito Otake - strongest NAIST imaging route

The Biomedical Imaging Intelligence lab is current and productive.

Its methodology includes:

- biomedical image understanding;
- deep learning;
- statistical modelling;
- Bayesian inference;
- patient-specific simulation;
- big-data mining;
- treatment planning.

Recent 2025-26 work includes volumetric MRI analysis and AI-based opportunistic assessment from CT/X-ray data.

#### Credible research pitch

> **Uncertainty-aware patient-specific anatomical modelling under heterogeneous and limited imaging data, with robust evaluation across acquisition settings.**

This leverages the VinBigData imaging experience but avoids a generic "medical image classification" proposal.

A stronger angle combines:

- domain shift;
- uncertainty;
- limited annotations;
- statistical/Bayesian modelling;
- reproducible evaluation.

**Decision: second NAIST contact if Fujiwara does not accept / is not available.**
