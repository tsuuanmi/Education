# Cross-Domain PI / Lab Frontier - First Audit

## Audit rule

This PI audit uses the canonical programme-fit specification.

A PI is **not** preferred merely because the lab repeats the applicant's existing domain.

The stronger question is:

> Can this lab use one or more existing strengths as leverage while developing deeper, transferable methods and giving the applicant ownership of a substantial research problem?

Priority therefore increases when a lab provides:

- rigorous modelling / inference / algorithms / scientific computing;
- engineering identity applied to biological systems;
- research ownership suitable for a Master's thesis;
- methods transferable beyond one modality;
- a coherent way to explain prior signals, imaging, genomics and software experience.

Min Hyuk Lim's Intelligence and Control-based BioMedicine lab is an unusually strong match to the target identity.

The lab explicitly combines:

- medical informatics / medical data science;
- time-series data;
- statistics;
- control;
- optimization;
- machine learning and AI;
- data-based and knowledge-based models;
- prediction, inference, decision-making and implementation.

This is almost a direct realization of the new programme-fit spec.

It preserves the applicant's systems/control identity while generalizing beyond EEG or genomics.

**Contact priority: P0.**

Kyemyung Park is the strongest UNIST cross-modality modelling alternative: the lab integrates multi-omics, clinical data and images using ML, Bayesian statistics and mathematical modelling.

Semin Lee is the strongest direct genomics route and would be an excellent GeneStory/DNA amplifier, but carries more specialization risk.

### NAIST - two unusually strong but different matches

**Koichi Fujiwara / Biomedical Data Science** is one of the most applicant-specific discoveries so far.

The lab explicitly studies difficult small biomedical datasets and argues that useful analysis requires:

- cleaning and aligning data formats;
- integrating physics/physiology and expert knowledge;
- collecting hard-to-obtain clinical data;
- developing diagnostic algorithms and medical devices.

Current domains include epilepsy, sleep disorder, stroke and other clinical data.

This is highly aligned with the applicant's Earable + scientific-data-engineering experience and teaches a method layer portable beyond neurotechnology.

**Contact priority: P0, subject to confirming 2027 IPGP eligibility.**

**Yoshito Otake / Biomedical Imaging Intelligence** is the strongest imaging-methods route. It combines biomedical imaging with deep learning, statistical modelling, Bayesian inference and patient-specific simulation.

Shigehiko Kanaya / Computational Systems Biology is a strong omics/informatics alternative.

### HKUST - three complementary ways to use the profile

**Jiguang Wang** is the strongest computational biological-information PI. His official profile lists cancer genomics and bioinformatics; HKUST describes his work as combining mathematics, big data and AI with biology.

**Terence Wong** is the strongest imaging/instrumentation PI, with medical imaging systems and bioinstrumentation and current AI-assisted pathology translation.

**Angela Wu** bridges genomics, systems biology, single-cell methods and microfluidic engineering.

This is exactly why HKUST's programme-level architecture is so valuable: PI choice can locate the thesis at different points of the same biological-information engineering identity.

### KAIST - choose between methods branches, not simply "neuro"

**Young-suk Lee** is the strongest computational genomics candidate: bioinformatics, functional genomics and computational molecular biology, with prior work on genome-wide disease characterization and cross-platform public-data integration.

**Sung-Hong Park** is a particularly interesting cross-modal methods lab: MRI acquisition/processing, ML, EEG validation and systems-oriented physiological imaging.

These two labs represent two distinct but coherent futures:

```text
Young-suk Lee
  GeneStory / DNA
      -> computational genomics methods

Sung-Hong Park
  Earable + VinBigData
      -> imaging / signals / ML / systems
```

Neither should automatically dominate; the final choice depends on which methodological depth the applicant wants.

### KAUST - strongest methods-specialized environment

**Xin Gao** is currently the clearest PI-level fit in KAUST: ML, algorithms and optimization applied from sequence analysis to structures, biological networks and healthcare.

This is exactly the desired model of using biology as application domain while deepening transferable computation.

**Robert Hoehndorf** is unusually interesting for biological-data infrastructure and knowledge representation.

**Jesper Tegner** provides a broader computational medicine / genomics / causality / AI route.

KAUST's new Bioinformatics Platform strengthens the surrounding environment further: it explicitly targets reproducible/scalable workflows, FAIR data, short/long-read processing, spatial omics, metagenomics and scientific software/infrastructure.

### Toronto - ecosystem breadth matters more than one perfect PI

**José Zariffa** is the strongest immediate continuation of Mechatronics + Earable: wearable sensors, neural interfaces, deep learning and computer vision in rehabilitation.

**Berj Bardakjian** is the strongest systems/control route: analysis, modelling and control of neural electrical rhythms plus ML.

**Leo Chou** shows the opposite side of Toronto's option value: DNA nanotechnology, molecular circuitry and computational design under the same BME degree.

Toronto therefore remains valuable as a platform even though no single PI necessarily spans every prior modality.

## Next pass

For Tier 0 and Tier 1 candidates, the next audit should answer five concrete questions:

1. Is the PI actively taking Master's/research students for the target cycle?
2. What are the last 3-5 years of lab projects/papers, and do they show methodological depth rather than only domain proximity?
3. What concrete research proposal could the applicant credibly pitch using existing evidence?
4. Does the lab have enough software/data/research infrastructure to support a Research Engineer trajectory?
5. Is funding attached to the PI/lab/programme, and what is the application/outreach timing?

This turns PI fit from a thematic label into an actionable application decision.
